Optimal Route Planning for Orienteering: Branch-and-Cut with Terrain Cost Surfaces and Fatigue

Preprint — submitted to European Journal of Operational Research, 2026

Orienteering is a sport where a runner must visit a set of checkpoints within a time budget. When time is limited, a selection problem arises: which checkpoints to visit and which to skip. This is the Orienteering Problem (OP). Most of the literature assumes symmetric travel costs, an assumption that breaks down on real terrain. In this paper we build a GIS-based cost surface from real terrain data using the Minetti metabolic model, introduce cumulative athlete fatigue as a position-dependent cost component, and formulate the resulting Asymmetric Orienteering Problem with Fatigue (AOPF). To solve it exactly, we develop two MILP formulations (one flow-based, one MTZ-based with McCormick linearization) and a branch-and-cut solver equipped with tailored cutting planes, including a novel family derived from the assignment relaxation. We validate the framework on real Spanish terrains, where the solver proves optimality on the majority of instances, and we release a parameterised benchmark suite of synthetic instances — the first for this problem variant.
